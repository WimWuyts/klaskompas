# Universal Classroom Expectations v0.2

**Status:** first school-aligned design version  
**Audience:** second-grade pupils in English and Spanish lessons  
**Design goal:** six short expectations that remain the same across classes while routines and support can vary.

## Design principles

The expectations must be:

- short enough to remember;
- observable rather than psychological;
- teachable through routines;
- compatible with the school regulations;
- restorative rather than permanently labelling;
- usable in both English and Spanish lessons.

## 1. We start and finish according to the routine

### Pupil-facing meaning

We enter calmly, go to the assigned place, prepare the required material, begin the start task and wait for the teacher's signal before packing away or leaving.

### Observable examples

- entering quietly and taking the assigned seat;
- placing a phone in the numbered pouch when required;
- having the correct book, notebook and writing material ready;
- beginning the displayed start task;
- closing books and tidying only on the teacher's signal.

### Not automatically a violation

- a one-off material problem that is reported immediately;
- arriving late with official Oase registration;
- an agreed accommodation or support measure.

## 2. We work actively and follow reasonable instructions

### Pupil-facing meaning

We listen to the task, start within the agreed time, complete the assigned work and ask for clarification when something is not possible or not understood.

### Observable examples

- beginning the first step of a task;
- taking notes when instructed;
- following the agreed individual, pair or group-work format;
- reporting a technical or material problem;
- returning attention after the teacher's signal.

### Not automatically a violation

- asking a genuine content question;
- making a mistake;
- needing an instruction repeated or broken into smaller steps;
- using an approved alternative method.

## 3. We let everyone learn

### Pupil-facing meaning

One person speaks at a time during instruction. We use the agreed working volume, remain in the assigned place unless we have permission and do not deliberately distract others.

### Observable examples

- waiting for a turn to speak;
- keeping side conversations for authorised collaboration;
- using the set voice level;
- not making noises or movements intended to attract attention;
- allowing classmates to concentrate and answer.

### Not automatically a violation

- one brief spontaneous reaction that stops after a reminder;
- productive discussion during pair or group work;
- movement that has been authorised or is part of an accommodation.

## 4. We treat people respectfully and keep everyone safe

### Pupil-facing meaning

We use polite language, respect differences, resolve disagreements without insults, threats or aggression and follow safety instructions immediately.

### Observable examples

- disagreeing without insulting;
- stopping when someone indicates a boundary;
- respecting another person's beliefs, background and identity;
- reporting bullying, violence or unsafe behaviour;
- following emergency and laboratory instructions.

### Serious behaviour

Violence, credible threats, bullying, sexual boundary violations, discriminatory/racist behaviour and dangerous objects do not build up through the ordinary warning ladder. They are stopped and escalated immediately.

## 5. We use material, technology and spaces only as agreed

### Pupil-facing meaning

We use books, school property, laptops and other devices for the assigned purpose, report damage immediately and do not use smart devices unless the teacher has authorised it.

### Observable examples

- opening only the required app or website;
- no games or social media without permission;
- handling the school laptop carefully;
- reporting a defect at the beginning of or during the lesson;
- keeping the workplace and classroom orderly;
- not eating, drinking or chewing gum during lessons unless permission is given.

### Not automatically a violation

- an accidental technical problem reported immediately;
- teacher-authorised device or AI use;
- an approved assistive technology.

## 6. We take responsibility and repair what goes wrong

### Pupil-facing meaning

We respond to correction, tell the truth about what happened, complete a reasonable repair action and show through later behaviour that the situation has been closed.

### Observable examples

- correcting behaviour after a reminder;
- returning or restoring material;
- completing missed clean-up or work time;
- taking part in a calm follow-up conversation;
- following an agreed improvement plan;
- acknowledging the effect on another person without a forced public apology.

### Not automatically a violation

- respectfully explaining a different view after the lesson;
- asking for help with a repair action;
- declining a formal restorative meeting that requires voluntary participation, while still following the school's formal process.

## Relationship to the school regulations

These six expectations condense the regulations; they do not replace them. Formal school rules on attendance, lateness, devices, safety, order measures and disciplinary procedures remain authoritative.

## Display version for pupils

```text
1. Start and finish according to the routine.
2. Work actively and follow reasonable instructions.
3. Let everyone learn.
4. Treat people respectfully and keep everyone safe.
5. Use material and technology only as agreed.
6. Take responsibility and repair what goes wrong.
```
